nil release notes
=================

0.1.1
-----

- The library keeps itself up to date: music added to or removed from your
  music folders shows up a few seconds after the copy finishes, without
  pressing Rescan. Only the changed files are read.
- Only one nil runs at a time. Starting it again brings the open window to
  the front, restoring it if it was minimized.
- nil.exe no longer contains folder paths from the machine it was built on.

0.1.0 — first release
---------------------

Playback
- Bit-perfect output through WASAPI exclusive mode, event-driven.
- FLAC (including MQA, untouched), WAV, AIFF, ALAC, DSF and DFF.
- DSD as DoP, limited to the rates the DAC actually accepts.
- AAC-LC, decoded to 24-bit with gapless trimming, marked not bit-perfect.
- Gapless playback between tracks.
- A track the DAC can't play as it is gets skipped with the reason, never
  converted.
- Device switching mid-track continues at the same position; plugging in or
  unplugging a DAC updates the device list straight away.

Library
- Scans your music folders in parallel and keeps an index, so later launches
  open instantly and only changed files are read again.
- Albums grouped by tags and folders, including CD1/CD2 folders and tagged
  compilations.
- Covers from the files or from folder.jpg / cover.jpg / front.jpg.
- Settings lists the files that couldn't be read, and why.

Interface
- Home with your most recently added albums; Albums, Artists and search.
- Album pages with track lists and formats.
- A floating now-playing bar with a live spectrum, the bit-perfect badge and
  the device picker; a full-window Now Playing view with the queue.
- Settings with your DAC's full capability table.
- A black, neutral theme with nil's own title bar.

Known limits
- No conversion fallback yet: DSD256 on a DAC that stops at DSD128, or a
  sample rate the DAC lacks, is skipped rather than converted.
- No volume control in nil, by design: set the volume on the DAC or amp.
- Windows 11's snap-layout menu doesn't appear over nil's maximize button;
  snapping by dragging and Win+arrow keys still work.
- Lossy formats other than AAC-LC (MP3, Opus, HE-AAC) are not supported.
